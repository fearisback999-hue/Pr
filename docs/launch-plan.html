Why $150 a test and not $250 At $250 the same $2,000 drops to a 1.5-month runway and capital starts throwing undercapitalization warnings. More shots at $150 beats fewer at $250, because what you are buying this month is attempts, not outcomes. And holding daily ad spend near $15 is the single number keeping the runway at three months — scale spend comes out of a winner's revenue, never out of the loan.

Five rules that don't bend

Everything else in this document is a suggestion about sequence. These are not.

  1. The 48-hour kill timer wins every argument. Below break-even for 48 straight hours is KILL, even when you feel it turning. validate makes the call; you execute it.
  2. No landed cost, no score. The engine refuses to score economics on a guessed supplier cost. Hold yourself to the same rule.
  3. Hold the product before you fund it. Photos lie. A quality problem found by a customer costs a refund spiral; found by you it costs $20.
  4. Log spend and revenue every day a test runs. Skipping one day breaks the consecutive-day math the kill timer depends on.
  5. AIGC disclosure on everything. export-creatives blocks any asset missing it, on purpose.

What month one is actually for

Expected value for month one is negative by design — month-one puts it near −$385 at this size. You are not buying profit. You are buying real landed costs and real conversion data on three real products, a working shop and ads and supplier pipeline, reps at the one skill that compounds (killing losers fast), and an option on a winner — which is where all the money actually is. Most first tests lose. That's the base rate, not a verdict on you.

Month 1 $2,000 finds a product that clears break-even. Expect to lose a few hundred dollars learning which one it is.
Months 2–3 Reinvest the winner's profit into scaling that product. This is where the first real money appears — not in month one.
Months 4+ A portfolio of about three winners with $25k+ ceilings each, funded by retained profit rather than by the loan.

scale itemizes the $100k month honestly: ~74 orders a day at $45 AOV, about three concurrent winners, ~$20k/mo in ads, 60+ active affiliates, ~30 videos a week — and $41,783 of working capital just to run that month. That number is the point. $2,000 does not buy a $100k month; it buys the first rung, and each rung is funded by the one below it. Skipping a rung by borrowing more is how this goes badly. The loan gets you onto rung one. That's its whole job, and it is enough for that job.
